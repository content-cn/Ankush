# Harborline Customer Support Policy Handbook

Version 6.1. Effective for all support interactions handled by Harborline Foods Ltd.

Harborline is a subscription grocery and meal-box service operating in the UK, Ireland and the Netherlands. This handbook is the authority for every support decision. Agents cite section numbers when explaining an outcome to a customer. Where an agent believes the correct outcome is not covered here, section 16 governs.

---

## 1. Scope and authority

### 1.1 Who this applies to

This handbook applies to every support interaction across every channel: email, live chat, telephone, and social media direct messages. It applies equally to agents, team leads, and any automated assistant drafting a reply on the company's behalf.

### 1.2 Customer types

Harborline recognises three customer types, and several sections behave differently for each.

- **Household.** An individual consumer on a personal subscription. The default. Full consumer protections apply.
- **Business.** A registered company account, typically a cafe, small restaurant, or office. Consumer protections do not apply; the commercial terms in section 12 govern instead.
- **Partner.** A reseller or distribution partner operating under a separate signed agreement. Nothing in this handbook applies to partners. Route to the partner desk, section 15.4.

Where an account type is unclear, treat it as Household until confirmed. Applying commercial terms to a consumer is a compliance risk; the reverse is only a cost.

### 1.3 Precedence

Where two sections appear to conflict, the more specific section governs. Where any section conflicts with a statutory consumer right, the statutory right governs and the agent escalates under section 15.2 so the handbook can be corrected. Where a section conflicts with a documented account-level exception recorded on the customer record, the exception governs for that account only.

### 1.4 Tone

Replies are plain, direct, and free of blame. Agents do not speculate about causes they cannot verify. Agents do not apologise for outcomes that were correct; they explain them. Where Harborline is at fault, the agent says so once, clearly, and moves to the remedy.

Agents never tell a customer what another department "should" have done, and never characterise a colleague's decision as a mistake to a customer.

## 2. Service hours and response times

### 2.1 Support hours

Live chat and telephone support operate Monday to Friday, 07:00 to 20:00, and Saturday and Sunday, 08:00 to 17:00, local time in the customer's registered country. Email is monitored during the same hours.

Outside these hours, the automated assistant handles account queries, delivery tracking, and subscription changes. Anything involving a refund, a credit, or a complaint is queued for the next working period rather than answered automatically.

### 2.2 Response targets

- Live chat: first response within 2 minutes.
- Telephone: answered within 90 seconds.
- Email: substantive reply within one working day.
- Complaints under section 14: acknowledgement within one working day, resolution within five working days.

A "substantive reply" means an answer or a decision. An acknowledgement that the query has been received does not satisfy the target and agents should not send one in place of an answer.

### 2.3 Public holidays

Support operates reduced hours on public holidays in each country, published on the help centre. Delivery does not operate on public holidays, and any delivery scheduled for one is moved to the following day automatically, with notification sent 48 hours ahead.

Where a policy deadline falls on a public holiday, the deadline extends to the next working day. This applies to claim windows, cancellation notice periods, and payment due dates.

## 3. Accounts

### 3.1 Account creation and ownership

An account belongs to the named account holder. The account holder is the person whose name appears on the payment method at the time of registration.

Only the account holder may change the payment method, change the delivery address, cancel the subscription, or request account closure. Any of these requested by another person requires the account holder to confirm directly, through the account or from the registered email address.

### 3.2 Shared access

An account may nominate one additional contact who can manage deliveries, skip weeks, and update delivery instructions. The additional contact cannot change payment details, cancel, or close the account.

Two people may share one account. Two people may not merge two existing accounts; one must be closed and any remaining credit transferred under section 8.5.

### 3.3 Identity verification

Before disclosing any account information or making any change, the agent verifies identity. Verification requires the registered email address plus one of: the delivery postcode, the last four digits of the payment method, or the order number of a delivery in the last 60 days.

Verification is never waived, including where the caller is distressed, in a hurry, or claims to have verified previously in the same conversation on another channel. If verification fails, the agent explains what is needed and offers to send a secure link to the registered email.

### 3.4 Account closure

Account closure is permanent and irreversible. Order history, saved preferences, and any unused credit are lost. The agent must state this explicitly and obtain confirmation before proceeding.

Closure requests are actioned within one working day. Any active subscription is cancelled at the same time under section 6, and the notice period in 6.2 still applies, meaning a final delivery may still be dispatched and charged.

### 3.5 Data requests

Requests for a copy of personal data, correction of personal data, or erasure are never handled by support directly. They are routed to the privacy desk under section 15.3 the same working day. The statutory response period is one calendar month and the clock starts on receipt, not on routing, so delay in routing consumes the company's own response window.

## 4. Subscriptions

### 4.1 Plans

Harborline offers three subscription plans. Each is billed on a recurring weekly basis.

- **Essentials.** A fixed box, no substitutions, lowest price point.
- **Flex.** A configurable box with substitutions permitted up to the cutoff in 5.2.
- **Market.** Fully customisable, minimum order value applies under 7.3.

### 4.2 Changing plan

A customer may change plan at any time. The change takes effect from the next billing cycle, not the current one, because the current cycle's box has already been allocated in the fulfilment system.

There is no limit on plan changes and no fee. An agent should not discourage a customer from downgrading; retention pressure applied at the point of a downgrade request is a conduct issue, not a sales technique.

### 4.3 Skipping deliveries

Any subscription may skip up to 8 deliveries per rolling 12-month period at no cost. Skips must be requested before the cutoff in 5.2. A skipped week is not billed.

Beyond 8 skips, further skips are permitted but the agent should discuss whether a pause under 4.4 or a plan change under 4.2 better suits the customer's pattern. The 8-skip limit is not enforced punitively; no account is ever charged for a skip or refused one.

### 4.4 Pausing

A subscription may be paused for a minimum of two consecutive weeks and a maximum of twelve. During a pause the account is not billed and no deliveries are dispatched. The subscription resumes automatically on the stated date unless the customer extends the pause before it ends.

A pause beyond twelve weeks is not available. The correct action is cancellation under section 6, and the customer can resubscribe later. Any promotional pricing does not survive cancellation, and the agent must state this before the customer decides.

### 4.5 Gift subscriptions

Gift subscriptions are prepaid for a fixed number of deliveries and do not auto-renew. The recipient controls delivery scheduling but cannot change the payment method, because there is none attached.

A gift subscription cannot be refunded once the first delivery has dispatched. Before that point it is fully refundable to the purchaser, not the recipient.

## 5. Deliveries

### 5.1 Delivery windows

Standard delivery is a four-hour window on the customer's chosen day, communicated the evening before. A one-hour narrowed window is sent on the morning of delivery.

Named-day delivery is available in all serviced postcodes. Timed delivery is not offered on any plan, and agents should not imply otherwise.

### 5.2 Cutoff times

Changes to an upcoming delivery, including skipping, substituting, changing the address, and changing the delivery day, must be made before **12:00 on the second day before delivery**. After the cutoff the box is packed and no change is possible.

This cutoff is absolute and is not an agent discretion. A request made after the cutoff cannot be actioned by anyone, including a team lead, because the fulfilment system has already committed the order.

### 5.3 Failed deliveries

Where nobody is present, the driver leaves the box in the customer's nominated safe place. Where no safe place is nominated and no neighbour accepts, the box is returned to the depot.

A returned box is not redelivered, because the contents are perishable. The customer is charged in full for the first occurrence in any 12-month period, and the agent explains the safe-place option. For a second occurrence in the same period, the agent should confirm the delivery instructions on file are correct before applying the charge.

Where the failure was caused by the driver, for example an incorrect address entered by Harborline, an inaccessible route not flagged, or a missed attempt logged incorrectly, the customer is not charged and a full credit is applied under section 8.

### 5.4 Late deliveries

A delivery arriving after the four-hour window is late. Where a delivery is more than two hours late, the customer is entitled to a credit of the delivery charge. Where a delivery arrives after 22:00 or does not arrive at all on the scheduled day, the customer is entitled to a full credit of the order value.

Lateness caused by severe weather, road closure, or industrial action is handled identically. Harborline does not distinguish between causes when compensating a customer, because the customer's experience is the same. Cause matters for internal reporting only.

### 5.5 Address changes

A delivery address change made before the cutoff in 5.2 takes effect immediately. A permanent address change outside a serviced postcode ends the subscription; the agent checks coverage before making the change and explains the consequence if the new address is not covered.

### 5.6 Delivery instructions

Safe-place instructions are stored on the account and apply to every future delivery until changed. Instructions that require the driver to enter a building, use a key, or contact the customer by phone cannot be honoured and should not be recorded.

## 6. Cancellation

### 6.1 Right to cancel

A subscription may be cancelled at any time, from the account or by contacting support. No reason is required and none should be requested beyond a single optional feedback question.

### 6.2 Notice period

Cancellation takes effect after the current billing cycle. Where cancellation is requested before the cutoff in 5.2, the upcoming delivery is not dispatched and not charged. Where cancellation is requested after the cutoff, the upcoming delivery is dispatched and charged, and cancellation takes effect after it.

This is the single most common source of dispute in the queue. The determining fact is the timestamp of the cancellation request against the cutoff, not the customer's intention, not when they first tried, and not when the confirmation email was generated. Confirmation emails are sent by a batch process and their timestamp can lag the request by up to two hours, so the confirmation email timestamp is never the deciding evidence. The agent checks the request timestamp on the account record.

### 6.3 Cooling-off period

Household customers cancelling within 14 days of first signup are entitled to a full refund of any amount charged, including for a delivery already received, provided the goods are unused. Perishable goods are exempt from the statutory return requirement, so Harborline does not require the box to be returned; the refund is issued regardless.

Business customers under section 12 have no cooling-off period.

### 6.4 Reactivation

A cancelled account may be reactivated within 90 days with history and preferences intact. After 90 days the account data is deleted and the customer registers afresh.

Promotional pricing does not survive cancellation and is not reinstated on reactivation. This applies even where the cancellation was made in error and reactivated the same day. Agents must not promise otherwise; the pricing is applied by the billing system at signup and cannot be reapplied manually.

## 7. Billing and payment

### 7.1 Billing cycle

Subscriptions are billed on the second day before delivery, at the same time as the cutoff in 5.2. The charge is for the upcoming delivery only. Harborline never bills in advance for more than one delivery, with the exception of gift subscriptions under 4.5.

### 7.2 Payment methods

Harborline accepts major debit and credit cards, and direct debit in the UK and Netherlands. Prepaid cards, cash on delivery, and bank transfer are not accepted.

The payment method is changed only by the account holder under section 3.1. Where a card has expired and a delivery is due within 48 hours, the agent may take the delivery out of the automatic dunning process for one cycle to give the customer time, and must note the account.

### 7.3 Minimum order value

The Market plan has a minimum order value of GBP 35, EUR 40, or the local equivalent. Below the minimum the order cannot be dispatched and the customer is notified at the cutoff.

Delivery is free on all orders above the minimum. There is no separate delivery charge on Essentials or Flex.

### 7.4 Failed payment

A failed payment triggers three retry attempts over five days. The upcoming delivery is held, not cancelled, during this period. If all three fail, the subscription is paused automatically and the customer is notified at each stage.

An account paused for failed payment is never closed automatically. Closure requires a customer request under 3.4.

### 7.5 Late payment

Household accounts incur no late payment fee. Business accounts under section 12 are subject to the terms in 12.4.

### 7.6 Price changes

Price changes are notified at least 30 days before they take effect. A customer who cancels within that 30-day window is not charged at the new price for any delivery, including one already scheduled.

### 7.7 Invoices and receipts

Receipts are issued automatically after each charge and are available in the account for 24 months. VAT invoices are available to Business accounts only, and are issued monthly rather than per delivery.

### 7.8 Tax

All displayed prices include VAT at the applicable rate. Business account prices are displayed excluding VAT, and this difference is a frequent source of confusion when a customer converts from Household to Business.

## 8. Quality claims, credits and refunds

### 8.1 What can be claimed

A customer may claim for an item that is missing, damaged, spoiled, substituted without permission on a plan that does not allow substitution, or delivered outside its use-by date.

A customer may not claim for an item they simply did not like, an item they ordered by mistake, or a substitution correctly applied on a plan that permits substitutions.

### 8.2 Claim window

Claims must be raised within **48 hours of delivery**. This window is short because the goods are perishable and the customer's account of their condition cannot be verified afterwards.

Where the 48-hour window ends on a public holiday, section 2.3 extends it to the next working day. The handbook is otherwise silent on late claims, and section 16 governs.

### 8.3 Evidence

Claims up to GBP 15 are accepted on the customer's word without evidence. Above that, a photograph is requested. Where a customer cannot provide one, the agent may still accept the claim once per rolling 12-month period and notes the account.

Agents do not ask for evidence in a way that implies the customer is lying. The request is framed as helping the supplier identify the batch, which is also its real purpose.

### 8.4 Remedies

The remedy for a valid claim is a credit to the account equal to the value of the affected items. A replacement delivery is not offered, because a single-item redelivery is not operationally possible.

Where more than half the box value is affected, the customer may choose a full credit of the order value or a refund to the original payment method. Where the whole box is affected, a refund to the original payment method is offered by default.

### 8.5 Credits

Credits are applied to the account and consumed automatically against the next charge. Credits do not expire. Credits are not transferable between accounts, with the exception of an account merge under 3.2, which requires a team lead.

Credit cannot be withdrawn as cash. On account closure under 3.4, unused credit is forfeited, and the agent must state this before closing.

### 8.6 Refunds

Refunds go to the original payment method and take three to five working days to appear. Harborline cannot expedite this; the delay is at the card issuer.

A refund is never issued to a different payment method or a different person, regardless of the reason given.

### 8.7 Repeat claims

Where an account has raised claims on more than three deliveries in a rolling 12-month period, the agent continues to honour valid claims and flags the account for quality review. The flag investigates the supply chain to that postcode; it is not a fraud flag and must never be described to a customer as one.

Withholding a valid claim because an account has claimed before is a conduct issue.

## 9. Substitutions

### 9.1 When substitution occurs

Where an item is unavailable at packing time, Flex and Market boxes receive a substitute of equal or greater value from the same category. Essentials boxes are never substituted; the item is omitted and the customer is credited automatically.

### 9.2 Notification

Substitutions are notified in the delivery email sent the evening before. A customer who does not want a particular substitute can refuse it at the door at no cost, and the credit is applied automatically.

### 9.3 Dietary and allergen preferences

Recorded dietary preferences and allergen exclusions are honoured absolutely by the substitution engine. A substitution that violates a recorded allergen exclusion is a safety incident and is escalated under section 15.1 immediately, regardless of whether the customer consumed the item or reports any harm.

The agent does not offer a credit as the primary response to an allergen incident. The primary response is escalation. A credit follows, but offering it first reads as an attempt to close the matter.

### 9.4 Limits

Substitution preferences cannot be set per item, only per category. Agents should not promise item-level control; it does not exist in the system.

## 10. Promotions and referrals

### 10.1 Promotional codes

One promotional code may be applied per account, ever. Codes are not stackable and cannot be applied retrospectively to an order already placed.

### 10.2 Expired promotions

A code is valid until its stated expiry, defined as the moment the order is placed, not the moment the customer began browsing. Where a customer placed an order while a promotional page was still visible but after the code expired, the code does not apply.

The handbook does not authorise honouring an expired code. Where a customer presents a genuine grievance, for example the promotional email itself stated a later date than the code enforced, the agent escalates under section 15.2 rather than deciding.

### 10.3 Referrals

An existing customer receives account credit when a referred customer completes their third delivery. Both parties are notified. The credit is not issued earlier, because referral abuse is concentrated in accounts that cancel after one delivery.

Self-referral, including referral to another member of the same household at the same address, does not qualify. Where detected, the credit is reversed and the agent explains the reason plainly.

### 10.4 Loyalty pricing

Loyalty pricing is applied automatically after 26 consecutive billed deliveries. Skipped weeks do not break the streak. A pause under 4.4 does not break the streak. A cancellation does, and it does not resume on reactivation under 6.4.

## 11. Packaging and sustainability

### 11.1 Returns

Insulated liners and ice packs are collected on the next delivery. There is no charge for non-return and no obligation on the customer.

### 11.2 Recycling

All packaging is kerbside recyclable except the insulated liner, which requires the collection scheme in 11.1. Agents should not state that the liner is kerbside recyclable; it is not, and the claim has previously required correction.

## 12. Business accounts

### 12.1 Eligibility

A Business account requires a registered company number and a VAT number where applicable. Sole traders qualify. Individuals ordering for a home office do not, and should remain on a Household account.

### 12.2 Consumer protections do not apply

Business accounts have no cooling-off period under 6.3 and no statutory return right. The commercial terms signed at account opening govern.

Where an account holder disputes this, the agent confirms the account type on record before responding. Applying commercial terms to an account that is actually Household is a compliance failure, and the agent should verify rather than assume.

### 12.3 Volume terms

Business accounts ordering above the volume threshold move to negotiated pricing handled by the accounts team, not by support. Support does not quote, discount, or indicate what pricing might be available.

### 12.4 Payment terms

Business accounts may be granted 14-day payment terms by the accounts team. Late payment beyond the agreed terms incurs interest at the statutory rate and deliveries are suspended after 21 days.

### 12.5 Contract variations

Requests to vary the standard terms, including indemnity clauses, service level commitments, liability caps, and bespoke data processing terms, are not handled by support. They go to the accounts team under 15.4.

The handbook contains no commitments of this kind and an agent must not imply any exist. The honest answer to a customer asking whether a commitment is in place is that support cannot confirm it and the accounts team will respond.

## 13. Data, privacy and record keeping

### 13.1 What agents record

Every interaction is logged against the account with the outcome and the section cited. Notes are factual and are visible to the customer on request under 3.5. Agents write notes on the assumption the customer will read them.

### 13.2 Retention

Order records are retained for 24 months from the order date, then aggregated and anonymised. Support interaction records are retained for 24 months. Payment records are retained for seven years for tax purposes and this retention is not affected by an erasure request.

### 13.3 Disclosure

Account information is never disclosed to a third party, including a spouse, a parent, an employer, or a person claiming to act on the customer's behalf, without the account holder's direct confirmation under 3.1.

Requests from law enforcement are routed to the legal desk immediately and are never actioned by support.

### 13.4 Recording

Telephone calls are recorded and the customer is informed at the start. A customer who objects may continue on email or chat instead; the recording cannot be disabled mid-call.

## 14. Complaints

### 14.1 What counts as a complaint

A complaint is any expression of dissatisfaction where the customer indicates the outcome was unacceptable, or uses the word complaint. A quality claim under section 8 is not automatically a complaint; it becomes one if the customer disputes the remedy.

### 14.2 Handling

Complaints are acknowledged within one working day and resolved within five. The customer is given a reference and a named owner.

A complaint is never closed on the basis that the original decision was correct. It is closed when the customer has been given a full explanation and told what, if anything, will change.

### 14.3 Multi-issue complaints

Where a complaint spans several areas, for example billing, delivery and quality together, each element is assessed against its own section. Where the sections prescribe different remedies, the agent applies each remedy to its own element rather than choosing one remedy for the whole complaint.

Where the customer demands a single combined remedy that no section supports, the agent escalates under 15.2. The agent does not invent a combined remedy.

### 14.4 Escalation by the customer

A customer dissatisfied with a complaint outcome may request review by a team lead, and after that by the customer relations manager. Both routes are offered proactively at the point a complaint is closed, not withheld until demanded.

## 15. Escalation

### 15.1 Immediate escalation

The following are escalated immediately, ahead of any other handling, and the agent does not attempt a remedy first:

- Any allergen incident under 9.3.
- Any report of illness following a delivery.
- Any suggestion of foreign material in a product.
- Any threat of legal action or regulatory referral.
- Any media enquiry.
- Any suspected data breach.

### 15.2 Escalation for a decision

Where the handbook does not cover the situation, or where two sections conflict in a way section 1.3 does not resolve, the agent escalates to a team lead for a decision. The agent tells the customer that a decision is being sought and gives a timeframe.

Agents do not guess. An answer given confidently and wrongly is harder to correct than a short delay.

### 15.3 Privacy desk

All requests under 3.5 and 13.3 route here the same working day.

### 15.4 Accounts and partner desk

Business contract variations under 12.5, volume pricing under 12.3, and all partner matters under 1.2 route here.

## 16. Situations this handbook does not cover

### 16.1 The default position

Where a request is reasonable but falls outside every category named in this handbook, the agent does not refuse it and does not grant it. The agent escalates under 15.2 and tells the customer honestly that the situation is not covered by standard policy and a decision is being sought.

Inventing an outcome, even a generous one, creates an expectation the next agent cannot meet, and the customer will reasonably cite it later.

### 16.2 Precedent

An exception granted to an account in the past does not create an entitlement for the future, and the agent may say so plainly. Equally, the agent does not treat a past exception as evidence that the customer is trying it on.

Where a customer cites a past exception, the agent checks the account record, confirms what actually happened, and explains the current position with reference to the relevant section.

### 16.3 Conflicting information previously given

Where a customer was given incorrect information by Harborline, the agent acknowledges it directly, states the correct position, and considers whether the customer acted on the incorrect information to their detriment. Where they did, the agent escalates under 15.2 rather than deciding the remedy.

The agent does not blame the colleague, does not speculate about why the error occurred, and does not describe it as unusual.

### 16.4 Requests for guarantees

Harborline does not guarantee that a specific outcome will never recur, and agents must not offer such an assurance in writing or verbally. What can be committed to is what this handbook actually provides: the remedy for the outcome if it recurs, and the fact that the incident has been recorded.

### 16.5 Superseded versions

The version of this handbook in force at the time of the order governs that order. A customer citing an older published version is answered against the version current at their order date. Where the older version was more generous and the customer relied on it, section 16.3 applies.

## 18. Worked examples

These are real dispute patterns from the queue, resolved against the sections above. They exist because each one was previously answered inconsistently by different agents.

### 18.1 Cancelled before the cutoff, charged anyway

A customer cancels at 11:40, forty minutes before the 12:00 cutoff, and is charged. The confirmation email is timestamped 13:20.

The charge is wrong. Under 6.2 the determining fact is the request timestamp on the account record, which is 11:40, and the confirmation email lag of up to two hours is explicitly not evidence. The delivery is stopped if still possible and the charge reversed. Where the box has already dispatched, the customer is refunded in full and keeps the goods, because recovery of perishables is not attempted.

### 18.2 Claim raised on the third day

A customer reports spoiled items 62 hours after delivery. The 48-hour window in 8.2 has passed and no public holiday intervened.

The handbook is silent on late claims. Under 16.1 the agent does not refuse and does not grant. The agent escalates to a team lead, tells the customer the claim is outside the standard window and is being reviewed, and gives a timeframe. The agent does not tell the customer the claim is invalid, because that is a decision the handbook does not authorise the agent to make.

### 18.3 Three short deliveries, each below the evidence threshold

An account reports one missing item on each of three consecutive deliveries, each worth under GBP 15.

Each claim is valid under 8.1 and each is accepted without evidence under 8.3, because the threshold applies per claim, not cumulatively. The account crosses the three-delivery mark in 8.7, so the agent flags it for quality review. The agent does not mention the flag to the customer and does not treat the pattern as suspicious; 8.7 makes the flag a supply-chain investigation.

### 18.4 Two clauses appear to conflict on a Friday claim

A claim is raised on Friday evening. Section 8.2 gives a 48-hour window; section 2.2 gives five working days for a complaint resolution.

These do not conflict. 8.2 governs when the claim must be raised; 2.2 governs how long resolution takes once raised. Under 1.3 the more specific section governs its own question. The claim is in time if raised by Sunday evening, and resolution runs on working days from Monday.

### 18.5 A commercial kitchen on a Household account

A cafe contacts support about a quality claim and mentions in passing that the boxes are used commercially. The account is registered as Household.

The agent applies Household terms, because 1.2 requires treating unclear cases as Household and 12.2 warns that applying commercial terms to a consumer account is a compliance failure. The agent handles the claim, then separately raises the account type with the accounts team under 15.4. The agent does not withhold the remedy pending that review.

### 18.6 Customer wants a full refund and to keep the goods

Half a box arrives unusable and the customer states the remainder is useless without it, asking for a full refund and to keep what arrived.

Under 8.4, where more than half the box value is affected the customer chooses between a full credit of the order value or a refund to the original payment method. Keeping the goods is not a concession; 8.4 never requires return of perishables. So the request is grantable as written, provided the affected proportion genuinely exceeds half. Where it does not, the remedy is a credit for the affected items only, and the agent explains the threshold.

### 18.7 Expired code, promotional page still live

A customer places an order while a promotional landing page is still visible, but after the code's expiry.

Under 10.2 the code does not apply, and the handbook does not authorise the agent to honour it. If the promotional email itself stated a later date than the code enforced, that is a genuine grievance and the agent escalates under 15.2. Otherwise the agent explains 10.2 plainly and does not escalate.

### 18.8 Customer cites an older version of the terms

A customer quotes a handbook version downloaded a year ago which was more generous on the claim window.

Under 16.5 the version in force at the order date governs, so the current window applies to a current order. If the customer relied on the older version to their detriment, for example they deliberately waited because they believed the window was longer, 16.3 applies and the agent escalates rather than deciding.

### 18.9 Two agents gave different answers

A customer was told by one agent that a pause could run indefinitely, and by another that twelve weeks is the maximum.

Twelve weeks is correct under 4.4. Under 16.3 the agent acknowledges the incorrect information directly, states the correct position, and considers detriment: if the customer declined to cancel on the strength of the incorrect answer and has since been billed, escalate under 15.2. The agent does not identify or characterise the colleague who gave the wrong answer.

### 18.10 Customer asks for written assurance it will not happen again

Following a second late delivery, the customer asks for written confirmation that future deliveries will arrive on time.

Under 16.4 no such assurance is given. What the agent can commit to in writing is the remedy under 5.4 if it recurs, and the fact that the incident is recorded under 13.1. The agent states both plainly rather than deflecting, because a refusal without an alternative reads as evasion.
